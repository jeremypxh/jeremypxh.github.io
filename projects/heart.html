Adaptive Inverse DOE for Online Calibration of Cardiac Electrical Signaling

Xinghao Peng, Hui Yang

Complex Systems Monitoring, Modeling and Control Laboratory · The Pennsylvania State University

Overview

Cardiac electrical signaling arises from the coordinated gating of voltage-gated ion channels in individual cardiomyocytes. Biophysical electrophysiology models are increasingly used as digital twins for mechanism studies, safety pharmacology and personalized prediction. Most calibration workflows, however, remain offline: they use fixed voltage-clamp and whole-cell protocols, estimate parameters after data collection, and ignore the feedback available during an ongoing patch-clamp experiment. This can lead to unnecessary protocol runs and weak parameter identifiability.

This project builds an online calibration framework that synchronizes a digital twin with a physical cell in real time through adaptive inverse design of experiments (DOE).

How it works

  1. Paired protocols. The physical cell and its pre-fitted digital twin are driven by the same experimental protocol, and the gaps between measured and simulated traces form a protocol-weighted loss.
  2. Revised Adam optimizer. Model parameters are updated online after each protocol. Parameters whose gradients stay small relative to the others are detected automatically and given amplified learning signals.
  3. Adaptive inverse DOE. When some parameters stay poorly informed for several epochs, an offline sensitivity screening picks an additional protocol type to run alongside the current ones. Separately, every few rounds, protocol settings such as clamp voltages are re-spread over the range that maximizes a Fisher-like information criterion (D-optimality), keeping the number of steps and the workload unchanged.
  4. From channels to the whole cell. Channel-targeted protocols calibrate ion-channel submodels in parallel. The calibrated components are then integrated into a whole-cell model for evaluation and, when needed, refinement.

Framework

Each round applies a protocol to both twins, updates the digital twin from the signal discrepancies, and lets the adaptive inverse DOE decide which protocols to add and how to configure them.

Results

Simulation case studies on the sodium (Nav) and potassium (Kv) channel submodels and on the integrated ventricular cell model.

  • Sodium channel. Calibrating with a single protocol family lowers the loss but leaves some parameters badly identified, with errors up to 11% (activation protocol only) and 23% (inactivation protocol only). Adaptive protocol selection adds the second protocol at epoch 20 and brings all four shifted parameters to errors of 1.3% or less.
  • Potassium channel. Adaptive updates of the clamp voltages cut the mean parameter error from 1.23% with Adam to 0.28%, against 11.4% for Bayesian online calibration, at a similar computation time.
  • Robustness. Across 10 heterogeneous simulated cells, the discrepancy loss falls to near zero, and the calibrated population matches the reference on a held-out ramp protocol.
  • Whole cell. Under a two-pulse paced action potential protocol, the calibrated digital twin follows the cell population while the uncalibrated model deviates.
