Wind Power Grid Integration: From Ramp Prediction to Economic Dispatch

Xinghao Peng, with Yanting Li and Fugee Tsung

Shanghai Jiao Tong University · The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology

Paper 2024 Paper 2023 Paper 2022

Overview

Wind power is clean but variable. Short, steep swings in output, known as wind power ramp events, can upset the grid's supply–demand balance and cause wind curtailment and load shedding. Dispatch models also need wind power scenarios that capture correlations across wind farms and over time, and the resulting stochastic, multi-objective problems must be solved efficiently.

This project tackles all three: it predicts ramp events, generates realistic scenarios, and builds dispatch models that use the scenarios and respond to ramp events through demand response.

How it works

  1. Ramp event prediction (WP-GAT). Ramps are detected in historical data with the L1-SW algorithm and described with power, meteorological, SCADA and ramp-frequency features. A spatio-temporal wind propagation graph records the direction and timing of wind moving between sites, and a graph attention network with a modified loss predicts upcoming ramps.
  2. Scenario generation. Spatiotemporal covariance functions capture correlations between wind farms and within each farm over time, with parameters estimated from empirical contour plots and a grid search. Pair copulas model the joint distribution of forecast and actual power, and Gaussian-process inverse sampling produces day-ahead scenarios that follow the forecast trend. A related study builds multi-farm scenarios with vine copulas, two-stage spatio-temporal sampling and conditional quantile sampling.
  3. Economic dispatch. The generated scenarios are tested in a two-stage dispatch model on the IEEE 6-bus system. For a microgrid, the two-stage dispatch is solved with deep reinforcement learning (DDPG) and an adaptive action-noise decay strategy. A multi-objective model then adds demand response to handle ramp events, balancing cost, emissions and the gap between day-ahead plans and actual operation, and is solved with Pareto-front and fuzzy optimization methods.

Results

  • Ramp prediction. On turbine data from the Baidu KDD Cup 2022 SDWPF dataset and 20 wind farms from NREL's WIND Toolkit, WP-GAT improves the critical success index by about 3.2% over random forest, 3.1% over gradient boosting and 6.1% over SVM classifiers across lead times.
  • Scenario generation. On 25 wind farms from NREL's WIND Toolkit, the vine-copula method achieves better variogram scores than the benchmark models with a nearly identical energy score, and grouping the farms halves model-estimation time (73 to 36 minutes). The covariance-function scenarios were checked against several evaluation metrics.
  • Dispatch. After training over many scenarios, the reinforcement-learning agent makes effective dispatch decisions under uncertainty, and using demand response to absorb ramp events proved both feasible and economical.
